Why these three questions

Most hotel searches start with a star rating and a price filter, which is why so many of them end badly. Star ratings in India describe facilities on a checklist, not whether you will sleep. The three questions above are the ones that actually predict whether a stay works, because each maps to a complaint that recurs constantly and is almost never visible in a listing.

Noise

The most common regret, and the hardest to see in advance. Central and heritage properties sit on working streets, and a courtyard building can be silent on one side and unusable on the other at the same price. In wedding season, from roughly November through February, heritage hotels host functions with music and fireworks that run late. Ask which side of the building your room faces and whether a wedding is booked on your dates.

Hidden costs

Compulsory meal plans, gala dinner charges over the New Year, transfers billed per journey, service charges added by default. None of these are usually hidden in the sense of being concealed; they are simply not in the number you compared against. If this is the thing that irritates you, the true cost comparator exists for exactly that.

Marketing that does not survive contact

"Wellness" and "eco" are unregulated words. A serious wellness property runs structured multi-day programmes with resident practitioners; a spa menu with a river view is a different product at a similar price. The same split runs through eco-labelling, where genuine practice means solar heating, greywater treatment and local hiring, and the marketing version means a bamboo toothbrush.
