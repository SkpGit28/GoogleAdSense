What each of these is for

These exist because four questions come up constantly while planning a stay in India, and none of them are answered by a booking site. Each tool runs entirely in your browser. None of them send anything anywhere, and none of them ask who you are.

Working out what a booking really costs

The true cost comparator takes two advertised rates and shows what each becomes at checkout once tax and compulsory extras are added. It is the right tool when you have two specific candidates and the advertised prices are close, because the gap after tax is frequently not the gap you were comparing.

The trip budget calculator works one level up, across a whole itinerary, and is most useful before you have chosen anything. It exists mainly to make the intercity transport line visible, which is the line people underestimate.

Working out when to go

The seasonal rate checker covers four regions across all twelve months, with every combination written out rather than falling back on a generic answer. It tells you what the rate pressure is that month and what the cheap months cost you in weather, which is usually the actual decision.

Working out whether to believe the listing

The listing translator flags the standard euphemisms in a hotel description and says what each one generally signals. It is a reading aid rather than a verdict, and the page explains the pattern the words follow so you can spot it without the tool.

The dealbreaker matcher is the quickest way in if you have not settled on a destination. Three questions, then it points you at whichever guide is closest to what you actually care about.

All of this sits alongside the guides, which do the same job property by property, with the month each rate was checked stated on the page.
