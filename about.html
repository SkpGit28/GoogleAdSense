Editorial Standards & Independence

Prime Hotel Picks is an independent accommodation research publication. Our guides are built on rigorous comparative analysis, real-time tariff auditing, and objective evaluation, helping travelers make well-informed decisions without commercial PR bias.

Unlike sponsored travel blogs that trade flattering reviews for complimentary stays, every assessment here is conducted through transparent consumer research. Where pricing, amenities, or seasonal availability cannot be independently confirmed, the guide states so plainly rather than relying on promotional marketing claims.

  • Zero Sponsored Bias: No hotel pays to be included, and criticism or drawbacks are never removed on request.
  • Independent Tariff Auditing: Rates, taxes, and hidden charges are audited directly across multiple booking channels and quoted with the date checked.
  • Verified Guest Analysis: We analyze multi-month guest feedback patterns to identify recurring maintenance, location, or service realities.
  • Transparent Attribution: Every photograph is properly credited to its original photographer on our photo credits page.

How the research is actually done

For each property I work through the same list.

  1. The hotel's own published material. Room categories, sizes, check-in times, published rates, restaurant names, what is included. This establishes what the hotel says about itself.
  2. Rates across more than one booking platform, on the same day, for a comparable date. Single-platform rates are unreliable. Anything quoted on this site is a range, with the month I checked it stated next to the number.
  3. Guest reviews read in bulk, not cherry-picked. I am looking for complaints that recur across many months and many reviewers. One angry review about a cold breakfast tells you nothing. Forty reviews mentioning traffic noise on the same side of the building tells you a great deal.
  4. Location checked against a map. Distance to the station, the airport, the thing you actually came to see. Hotels are very good at describing themselves as central.
  5. What it costs to get in the door beyond the room rate: taxes, mandatory meal plans, airport transfers, whether the famous restaurant is open to people who are not staying there.

About the rates on this site

Indian hotel rates move a lot. A palace room in Udaipur can double between a wet September and the last week of December. Every rate on this site is a range rather than a single number, and every range carries the month it was checked.

If you are reading a guide months after that date, treat the numbers as a rough sense of where a property sits rather than a quote. Check current rates before you book. I update guides when I revisit them, and the revision date is at the top of each one.

How this site makes money

Two ways, both worth stating plainly.

Advertising. The site carries display advertising. Advertisers have no say in which hotels appear or what is written about them, and I do not see which hotel an ad is promoting before publishing a guide.

Affiliate links. Some booking links may earn a commission if you book through them, at no extra cost to you. Where that applies it is disclosed on the page. A hotel does not get included, excluded, praised or criticised on the basis of whether a commission exists. Several properties recommended here have no affiliate programme at all, and at least one hotel in most guides is one I suggest you skip.
