Darjeeling splits into two very different travel experiences. One is congested Darjeeling town around Mall Road and Chaurastha with traffic jams, colonial bakeries, and views of Kangchenjunga. The other is rural tea estate bungalows set across terraced mountain slopes down in the valleys.

Deciding where to stay depends on whether you want market walks and Toy Train rides or quiet plantation life. Reaching valley tea estates like Glenburn or Makaibari requires driving one to two hours down narrow estate tracks off Hill Cart Road.

Rates listed below were checked in July 2026 across major booking channels, reflecting summer off-peak monsoon rates and autumn tea flush operating periods.

01

Glenburn Tea Estate

Rungeet Valley, 1.5 hours below Darjeeling town

Established by Scottish tea planters in 1859, Glenburn spans 1,600 acres of forest and tea gardens along the Rungeet and Kestrel rivers. The estate features two restored planter bungalows: The Burra Bungalow and The Water Bearer Bungalow.

Rooms feature four-poster teak beds, Venetian mirrors, period fireplaces, clawfoot bathtubs, and verandas looking out to Kangchenjunga peak. Rates operate on an all-inclusive plan covering all meals, estate tours, tea tastings, hikes, and vehicle transfers from town.

Indicative rates run from Rs 42,000 to Rs 88,000 per night for two guests. What you pay for is comprehensive hospitality and plantation access. The trade-off is distance. The estate sits 1,000 metres below Darjeeling town, making frequent trips into town impractical.

02

Mayfair Darjeeling and Town Heritage Stays

The Mall, opposite Governor's House, Darjeeling town

Formerly the summer residence of the Maharaja of Nazargunj, Mayfair Darjeeling sits opposite Raj Bhavan near Mall Road. It features hill-resort woodwork, period art, and manicured terrace lawns overlooking the valley.

Rooms feature dark wood paneling, fireplaces, and valley balconies. Rates range from Rs 14,000 to Rs 32,000 per night. Staying here gives you walking access to Chaurastha square, Keventer's open-air breakfast terrace, and Glenary's bakery.

The drawback in town is street congestion and parking bans along Mall Road. Taxis drop guests at designated stands, requiring short walks along pedestrian inclines.

03

Central Town Budget Stays on Chaurastha Inclines

Lower Club Side Road and Gandhi Road

This is the location to skip: Budget hotels on narrow Chaurastha stair alleys. Dozens of budget hotels along Gandhi Road or Club Side Road advertise "panoramic tea garden and mountain views". In reality, most budget rooms look straight into concrete walls or neighbor laundry lines.

Rates run from Rs 2,500 to Rs 6,800. Carrying heavy suitcases up steep staircases from taxi drop-offs on Cart Road in rain or mist makes arrival stressful.

If budget is tight, pick a guesthouse along HD Lama Road near the bus stand where taxi access is easier, or spend a night in Kurseong tea estates further down Hill Cart Road.

What it costs to actually get there

Reaching Darjeeling requires patience and budget. Flights land at Bagdogra Airport (IXB), 70 km down on the plains. From there, a private prepaid taxi to Darjeeling town costs between Rs 2,500 and Rs 3,500 depending on vehicle size and season. The steep, winding ascent via Rohini Road or Hill Cart Road takes approximately three hours in clear weather, but heavy traffic near Kurseong can push this to four hours.

Shared jeeps offer a budget alternative at roughly Rs 300 to Rs 400 per seat from the plains, but they cram ten passengers into a single vehicle, offering zero luggage space and a deeply uncomfortable ride up the mountain curves.

When to go, and what changes

Tea estates operate on distinct harvesting seasons called flushes. The First Flush (March to April) yields light, floral teas and coincides with clear spring skies, making it the most expensive time to visit. The Second Flush (May to June) produces the famous muscatel flavor, drawing peak summer crowds escaping the plains heat.

The monsoon from July to September brings relentless heavy rain and frequent landslides, often blocking the main highway for days. While tea estates turn incredibly lush and green during the rains, outdoor hikes become impossible due to mud and mountain leeches. If you visit in winter (December to February), confirm that your bungalow provides electric blankets or fireplaces, as temperatures drop near freezing at night.

Choosing your Darjeeling mountain base

If you want quiet tea garden walks, private tasting sessions, and total silence under starry mountain skies, book a tea estate bungalow like Glenburn or Makaibari. If you want morning walks to Keventer's, colonial church tours, and rides on the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, pick a town hotel like Mayfair.

Practical Darjeeling travel notes

  • Bagdogra Airport (IXB) and NJP Railway Station in Siliguri sit 68 km south of Darjeeling. The uphill drive via Hill Cart Road or Rohini Road takes roughly 3 hours.
  • Joy Rides on the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (steam Toy Train) between Darjeeling and Ghum sell out fast. Reserve seats online via IRCTC weeks in advance.
  • Tiger Hill sunrise viewpoint requires leaving your hotel by 4:00am. Traffic jams along the narrow summit road can be intense during October and May peak months.
  • Water scarcity is a winter reality across Darjeeling town hotels. Use shower water mindfully and confirm whether your hotel provides 24-hour hot water running taps.
