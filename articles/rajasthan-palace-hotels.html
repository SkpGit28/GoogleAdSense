Rajasthan has more former royal residences working as hotels than anywhere else in India, and the word "palace" on a booking page has stopped meaning very much. It gets used for genuine 1920s state residences and for four-year-old buildings with a scalloped arch over the porch.

These three are all real. One is a former maharaja's residence that the family still partly lives in, one is a hunting lodge that grew into a royal home, and one is an 18th-century townhouse in the old city of Jaipur. They also cost wildly different amounts, which is the part most write-ups skate over.

Rates below are indicative ranges, compared across booking platforms in July 2026. Palace rates in Rajasthan swing hard by season. A room that goes for one price in a wet August can be two or three times that in the last week of December. Treat these as a sense of where each property sits, not a quote.

01

Rambagh Palace, Jaipur

Bhawani Singh Road, about 5 km from the old city

Rambagh started in 1835 as a house for the queen's favourite handmaiden, became a royal hunting lodge, then the principal residence of the Maharaja of Jaipur, and has been a hotel since 1957. It is run by Taj. The grounds are large enough that peacocks wander across the lawns in the early evening without any obvious encouragement.

What you are paying for is the building and the space around it. The cheapest category, the Palace Rooms, are handsome without being enormous. Above that the Historical Suites are named after the people who lived in them and are a significant step up in both size and price. Suvarna Mahal, the Indian restaurant, is in what was the palace ballroom. There is a bar built into a restored railway carriage.

The honest criticism is that Rambagh is not walkable to anything. It sits in a well-off residential part of Jaipur, and the old city, Hawa Mahal and the bazaars are a drive. If your picture of Jaipur involves stepping out of the hotel into the noise, this is the wrong address. You will be getting into a car every time.

02

Umaid Bhawan Palace, Jodhpur

On the ridge above Jodhpur, roughly 5 km from the clock tower

Umaid Bhawan was built between 1928 and 1943 in golden-yellow chittar sandstone, and it is enormous. The building is split three ways: the royal family still occupies part of it, part is a museum open to day visitors, and the rest is a Taj hotel of around 64 rooms sitting in 26 acres of grounds.

The interiors are the interesting part. This is not a Rajput palace with mirror work and painted arches. It went up in the late 1920s and the inside is Art Deco, all long symmetrical corridors and period furniture, which is unusual in Rajasthan and is really the reason to come.

This is the one I would tell most readers to skip. Not because it is bad, but because of the number. Reported tariffs start around Rs 1.8 to 2.5 lakh a night for the heritage rooms and run to several times that for the signature suites. At that level you are not choosing between hotels, you are deciding whether to spend the price of a small car on somewhere to sleep. If you want to see the building, the museum is open to non-residents for a modest ticket and you can stay somewhere else in Jodhpur for a fraction of the difference.

03

Samode Haveli, Jaipur

Gangapole, inside the old city walls

Worth clearing up a common mix-up first. Samode Haveli is the family's townhouse inside Jaipur. Samode Palace is a separate, larger property about an hour and a half north in Samode village. People book one thinking they have booked the other. This entry is about the Haveli.

It is an 18th-century courtyard house at Gangapole, on the northern edge of the walled city, and it is the one on this page I would actually recommend to most people. Entry rooms start far lower than either palace above. The best-known room is the Sheesh Mahal suite, covered floor to ceiling in mirror and painted glass, and it costs a fraction of a mid-tier suite at Rambagh.

The location is the real argument for it. You are inside the old city. The bazaars, the City Palace and Hawa Mahal are a short walk or a very short ride rather than a twenty-minute drive across town.

The trade-off is that a heritage courtyard house is not a resort. Rooms vary a lot in size and light because the building was never designed as a hotel, so two rooms at the same rate can be quite different. Gangapole is a working part of the city and it is not silent. Light sleepers should ask for something off the street side.

Which one, and why

If you have one night in Jaipur and want a palace, go to Rambagh and accept that you will be in a taxi whenever you leave. If you have three nights and want to see the city rather than the inside of a hotel, Samode Haveli does that better and leaves you enough money to eat well.

Umaid Bhawan is a different proposition. It is worth seeing and it is worth the museum ticket. Staying there is a decision about money rather than about Jodhpur.

Booking notes for all three

  • Check whether the rate includes breakfast. At the palace properties it often does not, and adding it for two people for three nights is not trivial.
  • December and January are peak. If your dates are flexible, moving to February or late September changes the price more than choosing a different hotel does.
  • Ask what is closed. Heritage buildings run restoration work in the low season and the courtyard in the photographs is occasionally behind scaffolding.
  • At the palace hotels, confirm whether airport transfer is included or billed separately.
