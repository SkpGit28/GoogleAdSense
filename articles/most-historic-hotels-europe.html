Most Historic Hotels in Europe

Jun 12, 2026 By Mr. Lee 10 min read
Advertisement

Europe is home to some of the world's most historic hotels, properties that have witnessed centuries of history and hosted royalty, artists, and world leaders. Staying in these hotels is like stepping back in time while enjoying modern luxury.

In this guide, we've curated the most historic hotels across Europe, each offering a unique connection to the past while providing exceptional guest experiences.

What Makes a Hotel "Historic"?

A truly historic hotel typically features:

  • Age: At least 100 years old, often much older
  • Architectural significance: Original features and design elements
  • Famous guests: Historical figures, celebrities, or royalty
  • Cultural importance: Role in local or national history
  • Preservation: Maintained original character while adding modern comforts

Most Historic Hotels by Country

England

1. The Savoy, London

Opened in 1889, The Savoy was London's first luxury hotel and remains one of the most famous in the world. The hotel has hosted everyone from royalty to rock stars and features original Art Deco and Edwardian design elements.

Historic Highlights

  • Opened in 1889 as London's first luxury hotel
  • Original Art Deco and Edwardian design
  • Famous guests include royalty and celebrities
  • Historic American Bar and Savoy Grill
  • Thames riverfront location

France

2. Hôtel de Crillon, Paris

Overlooking Place de la Concorde, Hôtel de Crillon has been a Parisian landmark since 1758. The hotel occupies an 18th-century palace and has hosted countless dignitaries and celebrities.

Historic Highlights

  • Originally built in 1758
  • Overlooks Place de la Concorde
  • Hosted diplomatic negotiations
  • Original marble and crystal interiors
  • Recently restored to former glory

Italy

3. Hotel Danieli, Venice

Once the palace of a Doge of Venice, Hotel Danieli has welcomed guests since 1822. The hotel's Gothic architecture and stunning views of the lagoon make it one of Venice's most iconic properties.

Austria

4. Hotel Sacher, Vienna

Founded in 1876, Hotel Sacher is famous for the invention of Sachertorte. The hotel's opulent interiors and central location make it a Vienna institution.

Switzerland

5. Baur au Lac, Zurich

Opened in 1844, Baur au Lac has been Zurich's premier hotel for nearly two centuries. The hotel has hosted composers, politicians, and business leaders from around the world.

Scotland

6. The Balmoral, Edinburgh

This iconic Edinburgh hotel has been a landmark since 1902. The Balmoral's clock tower is set three minutes fast to help guests catch their trains, a tradition that continues today.

Tips for Staying in Historic Hotels

  • Ask about history: Staff often have fascinating stories about the property
  • Look for original features: Many hotels preserve architectural details
  • Check modern amenities: Historic doesn't mean outdated
  • Book heritage rooms: Some hotels offer rooms with original furnishings
  • Research famous guests: You might sleep in the same room as a celebrity

Conclusion

Europe's historic hotels offer a unique opportunity to connect with the past while enjoying modern luxury. These properties have stood the test of time and continue to provide exceptional experiences for discerning travelers.
