Every hotel desk in India asks for your ID, but front desk clerks routinely make up their own rules. A PAN card gets rejected because it lacks an address. A digital copy on your phone in DigiLocker is legally equivalent to an original card under Central law, yet receptionists still demand laminated plastic.

Hotel check-in rules in India are governed by specific Tourism Ministry guidelines, state police sarais acts, and Supreme Court rulings on privacy. Here is what the law actually requires, and what hotels cannot legally demand.

1. Statutorily Accepted Identity Proofs

Under Ministry of Tourism guidelines and state police hotel regulations (such as the Sarais Act and municipal police orders), every guest staying in a commercial accommodation must produce a government-issued photo identity document that verifies both identity and residential address.

Document Validity Status Important Conditions

Indian Passport Accepted Must be unexpired. Universally accepted across all hotel categories.

DigiLocker Validity: Under Rule 9A of the Information Technology (Preservation and Retention of Information by Intermediaries Providing Digital Locker Facilities) Rules, 2016, documents stored in the government DigiLocker application have the exact same legal status as original physical documents. Hotels cannot arbitrarily reject a DigiLocker-issued Aadhaar or Driving License.

2. Foreign Nationals and NRIs: The Form C Requirement

If you hold a foreign passport (including Overseas Citizen of India / OCI cardholders travelling on a foreign passport), Indian law imposes strict reporting obligations on accommodation providers under Section 7 of the Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939, and Rule 14 of the Registration of Foreigners Rules, 1992.

  • Online Submission within 24 Hours: Every hotel, guest house, and homestay must register foreign guests on the Bureau of Immigration's electronic Form C portal (indianfrro.gov.in) within 24 hours of arrival.
  • Mandatory Documents: Foreign guests must present their original Physical Passport and Valid Indian Visa (or e-Visa printout / OCI card). Front desks are required to record visa number, expiry, port of entry, and arrival date.
  • The Common Homestay Rejection: Many budget homestays and heritage guesthouses in remote parts of Himachal Pradesh, Ladakh, or rural Rajasthan lack an active FRRO/Form C hotel portal registration. As a result, they may abruptly turn away foreign guests at the door. If traveling on a foreign passport, always message independent properties in advance to confirm their active Form C portal account.

3. Unmarried Adult Couples: The Legal Position vs. Ground Reality

One of the most persistent sources of check-in harassment across India involves unmarried adult couples booking hotel rooms together.

The Statutory Reality

There is no law in India that prohibits two consenting adults (aged 18 or above) from renting and sharing a hotel room together.

The Supreme Court of India has repeatedly affirmed that consenting adults have a fundamental right to privacy and freedom of association under Article 21 of the Constitution. Neither the Indian Penal Code nor any Central Tourism statute requires guests to prove marital status or produce a marriage certificate.

Why do some hotels refuse check-in?

Under the principle of contract law, private hotels maintain the legal right to frame their own "house policies", provided they do not violate constitutional protections. Some conservative standalone properties enforce an internal "no unmarried couples" policy to avoid scrutiny from local police or moral policing groups.

How to avoid issues:

  1. Check the property's booking policy on OTAs before confirming. Filter specifically for "Couple Friendly" stays (curated on MakeMyTrip, Booking.com, and specialized platforms).
  2. Major branded hotel chains (Taj, Marriott, Accor, ITC, Hyatt, Lemon Tree) adhere strictly to central statutory guidelines and never request marriage certificates from adult guests with valid government IDs.

4. The "Local ID" Check-In Restriction

Travelers frequently notice hotel listings carrying the disclaimer: "Guests with local city IDs are not allowed." For example, a traveler holding a Delhi Aadhaar card attempting to book a boutique hotel in South Delhi for a weekend staycation is refused at the counter.

Hotels enact local-ID bans primarily as an internal risk management policy against illicit parties, underage gatherings, or potential police inspections. While not an explicit statutory mandate from the Central government, local municipal police departments sometimes issue advisory circulars discouraging budget hotels from admitting same-city guests without secondary verification. If planning a staycation in your home city, always select mid-market or upscale properties that explicitly permit local IDs.

5. Guest Rights During Your Stay

When you check into a registered commercial hotel in India, you are entitled to several statutory and consumer protections under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019:

  • Room Privacy and Quiet Enjoyment: Hotel staff cannot enter your occupied room without explicit permission, except in demonstrable medical or safety emergencies. Unannounced housekeeping entries violate your consumer rights.
  • Baggage Liability: Under Section 151 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, the hotel acts as a bailee for your property. While hotels display disclaimers absolving themselves of responsibility for room contents, hotels have been held legally liable in consumer forums for theft when in-room digital safes malfunctioned or electronic door lock access logs revealed unauthorized master-key entry.
  • Right to Full Bill Breakup: You are entitled to an itemized tax invoice. Front desks cannot refuse a printed bill showing room charges, restaurant orders, and taxes separately.

6. Check-In Grace Periods and Dispute Escalation

Standard hotel check-in across India typically begins between 12:00 PM and 2:00 PM, with checkout between 10:00 AM and 11:00 AM. If you arrive early, hotels are not statutorily obligated to provide an unoccupied room before the published check-in hour, though luggage storage at the bell desk is standard and free of charge.

If a hotel unlawfully refuses check-in for a prepaid booking:

  1. Demand a written note of refusal signed by the Duty Manager detailing the exact ground for denial. (Most managers will quickly find a room rather than put an unlawful refusal in writing).
  2. Contact the booking portal's emergency support line immediately to initiate an alternate accommodation placement or full refund.
  3. Register a formal consumer grievance through the National Consumer Helpline (Toll-Free 1915 or consumerhelpline.gov.in), citing unfair trade practices under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
