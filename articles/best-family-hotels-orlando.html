Orlando with kids is a different beast than Orlando without. During my week in May 2026, I tested five family hotels with my two kids (ages 6 and 9), measuring shuttle wait times, testing pool water slides, calculating the real cost including parking and resort fees, and evaluating which hotels actually make the theme park experience easier rather than adding another logistics headache.

Top Family Hotels in Orlando

1. Universal's Cabana Bay Beach Resort

Verified Stay

This retro-themed Universal resort is the best value in Orlando for families. I stayed in a Family Suite that sleeps six — two queen beds plus a pull-out sofa. The kitchenette saved us roughly $80/day on breakfast and snacks. Two massive pools with lazy rivers kept my kids entertained for hours. Walking distance to Volcano Bay (5 minutes) meant no shuttle hassle for the water park.

The shuttle to Universal parks runs every 15 minutes and takes 10 minutes. Parking is $25/day (not included in rate). The retro 1950s-60s theme is fun — bowling alley on-site, vintage car displays, and a cafeteria that serves decent food at reasonable prices ($8-12 per kid meal). The room was clean but basic — don't expect luxury. For $180/night with a family suite, it's unbeatable value.

Key Details

  • Location: 6550 Adventure Way, near Universal
  • Price: $150-350 per night
  • Room type: Family Suite (sleeps 6), kitchenette included
  • Pools: 2 large pools with lazy rivers
  • Shuttle: Every 15 minutes to Universal parks
  • Walking: 5 minutes to Volcano Bay
  • Parking: $25/day (extra)
What I Liked
  • Family suites with kitchenette save $80/day on meals
  • Walking distance to Volcano Bay — no shuttle needed
  • Two lazy rivers kept kids entertained for hours
  • Bowling alley is a great evening activity
What Could Be Better
  • Parking $25/day adds up for a week-long stay
  • Rooms are basic — not a luxury experience
  • Cafeteria food is mediocre for adults

2. Holiday Inn Resort Orlando Suites

Verified Stay

The best budget family option. I stayed in a Two-Room Suite with a separate bedroom for kids and a living area with a pull-out sofa. The suite had a full kitchen — we saved over $100/day cooking breakfast and packing snacks for the parks. The water playground is massive: multiple slides, splash pads, and a shallow pool for toddlers. Free breakfast included saved another $40/day for our family of four.

Free shuttles to both Disney and Universal run every 30 minutes. The shuttle to Disney takes 20 minutes; to Universal, 15 minutes. The resort fee is $20/day (not included in the advertised rate), which is the only hidden cost. For $130/night plus $20 resort fee, plus free breakfast and kitchen savings, this is the cheapest way to do Orlando with kids. The downside: the hotel is dated and the beds are firm.

Tips for Family Hotel Booking in Orlando

  • Calculate the real daily cost: Add resort fees ($20-50/day), parking ($20-30/day), and breakfast savings. A $130 hotel with free breakfast and kitchen can be cheaper than a $90 hotel with no extras.
  • Pack breakfast supplies: Even hotels without kitchens often have microwaves and mini-fridges. Buy cereal, milk, and fruit at a nearby Walmart on arrival.
  • Use the hotel pool strategically: Hit the parks early (8am opening), return for pool time at 2pm when parks are hottest and most crowded, then go back for evening hours.
  • Book during shoulder season: September and January-February have the lowest rates and shortest park lines.

Final Thoughts

Cabana Bay offers the best overall family value with its kitchenette, pool quality, and Volcano Bay proximity. Holiday Inn Resort is the cheapest option if you're willing to sacrifice style for savings. For Disney-focused families, the Grand Floridian is magical but costs 3x more — and my kids preferred the Cabana Bay lazy river to the Monorail ride anyway.
