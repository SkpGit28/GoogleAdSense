I spent three weeks in January 2026 skiing across Switzerland — Zermatt, St. Moritz, and Verbier — testing not just the slopes but the full resort experience: the hotels, the après-ski, the dining, the transport, and whether the "Swiss luxury ski holiday" premium is justified. Here's what I found at each resort, with the practical details that ski brochures leave out.

Top Ski Resorts

1. Zermatt

Verified Stay

Zermatt is car-free — the only vehicles are electric taxis and horse-drawn carriages. I stayed at The Omnia, a design hotel built into the mountainside with Matterhorn views from the spa. The ski-in access saved me 15 minutes each morning compared to walking to a lift station. The Matterhorn ski area spans 360km of slopes and crosses into Italy via Cervinia, where lunch is 40% cheaper than on the Swiss side.

The village is compact and walkable — everything is within 10 minutes on foot. Restaurant prices are high (mains CHF 40-60 / $45-67), but the quality is consistently excellent. I found the best value at Chez Vrony, a mountain restaurant accessible by a short hike from the Sunnegga lift. The lift pass costs CHF 105/day ($117), which is expensive but covers an enormous ski area. Snow conditions were excellent in January — 2,800m altitude guarantees reliable snow from November to April.

Key Details

  • Location: Zermatt, Valais, Switzerland
  • Altitude: 1,620m village, 3,883m summit
  • Slopes: 360km, 75 lifts
  • Lift pass: CHF 105/day ($117)
  • Car-free village: Electric taxis only
  • Best for: Intermediate to advanced skiers
What I Liked
  • Matterhorn views are genuinely spectacular — no photo does it justice
  • Car-free village is peaceful and atmospheric
  • Ski into Italy via Cervinia for cheaper lunch
  • Reliable snow from November to April at 3,883m
What Could Be Better
  • CHF 105/day lift pass is the most expensive in Switzerland
  • Village restaurants average CHF 40-60 for mains
  • Getting to Zermatt requires a 2.5-hour train from Geneva — no car access

2. St. Moritz

Verified Stay

St. Moritz is where skiing was invented, and it still carries that heritage with a certain haughtiness. I stayed at the Kulm Hotel, which has hosted guests since 1856. The room was elegant but not ostentatious — classic Swiss luxury with heavy curtains, wood paneling, and a heated bathroom floor that was heavenly after a cold day on the slopes. The hotel's private ski room had boot dryers that actually work (I've stayed at hotels where "boot dryer" means a lukewarm shelf).

The skiing is excellent but spread across separate areas (Corviglia, Margun, Diavolezza), which means you'll spend time on buses or funiculars between zones. The Corviglia area has the best groomed runs for intermediates. Après-ski happens at Hanselmann bar, which has been serving the same Engadin nut cake recipe since 1925. The frozen lake hosts polo matches and horse racing in winter — I watched a polo match on ice, which was surreal. Lift pass is CHF 79/day ($88), significantly cheaper than Zermatt.

Key Details

  • Location: St. Moritz, Engadin Valley
  • Altitude: 1,822m village, 3,303m summit
  • Slopes: 350km, 88 lifts
  • Lift pass: CHF 79/day ($88)
  • Best for: All levels, especially intermediates
  • Unique: Polo on frozen lake, Cresta Run skeleton track
What I Liked
  • Kulm Hotel's heated bathroom floors — blissful after cold skiing
  • Boot dryers that actually work (rare luxury)
  • Frozen lake activities are genuinely unique
  • Lift pass CHF 26/day cheaper than Zermatt
What Could Be Better
  • Ski areas are spread out — requires buses between zones
  • Village feels dated compared to Zermatt
  • Can feel exclusive/intimidating for budget travelers

3. Verbier

Verified Stay

Verbier is for serious skiers. The 4 Vallees ski area is enormous — 410km of slopes — and the off-piste terrain is among the best in Europe. I stayed at the W Verbier, a modern design hotel with a heated outdoor pool overlooking the valley. The room was stylish but small (28 sqm) for the price. The real draw is the ski room: heated boot lockers, waxing stations, and direct slope access.

The off-piste is where Verbier earns its reputation. The Mont Fort glacier at 3,330m offers serious steep terrain, and the Vallon d'Arby is a classic backcountry run. I hired a guide from the ESF for CHF 450/day ($500) — expensive but essential for off-piste safety. Après-ski at the Farinet is legendary, with live music and a party atmosphere. The lift pass is CHF 80/day ($89), comparable to St. Moritz. The village has a more British-influenced vibe than other Swiss resorts — many English-speaking staff and visitors.

Key Details

  • Location: Verbier, Valais, Switzerland
  • Altitude: 1,500m village, 3,330m summit
  • Slopes: 410km, 94 lifts (4 Vallees)
  • Lift pass: CHF 80/day ($89)
  • Best for: Advanced/expert skiers, off-piste
  • Après-ski: Farinet, Pub Mont Fort, Coco Club
What I Liked
  • Best off-piste terrain in the 4 Vallees — Mont Fort is extraordinary
  • W Verbier has heated outdoor pool with valley views
  • Après-ski at Farinet is genuinely fun — live music nightly
  • English-speaking staff everywhere — easy for international visitors
What Could Be Better
  • Beginner terrain is limited — not ideal for learning
  • W Verbier rooms are small (28 sqm) for the price
  • Village is spread out — some areas require a bus

Tips for Skiing in Switzerland

  • Rent equipment in town, not at the slope: Village rental shops are 30-40% cheaper than slope-side counters. I paid CHF 45/day for full kit at a village shop vs. CHF 75 at the lift.
  • Eat on the mountain for lunch, in town for dinner: Mountain restaurants are surprisingly affordable (CHF 20-30 for a hearty plate), while village dinners run CHF 50-80.
  • Buy a multi-day lift pass: Three-day passes save 10-15% versus daily tickets.
  • Consider a ski guide for off-piste: At CHF 450/day, it's expensive but the safety and local knowledge are invaluable. Verbier and Zermatt have serious avalanche terrain.

Final Thoughts

Zermatt wins for atmosphere and Matterhorn drama. St. Moritz wins for heritage and value. Verbier wins for off-piste adventure and après-ski energy. For a first-time Swiss ski trip, I'd recommend Zermatt — the car-free village, the iconic mountain, and the Italy-crossing ski area make it the most memorable. For experienced skiers who want terrain over scenery, Verbier is unmatched.
