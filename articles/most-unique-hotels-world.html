I've reviewed over 500 hotels in my career, but the ones I remember most vividly aren't the luxury chains — they're the properties that made me say "I've never experienced anything like this." Over the past three years, I've specifically sought out the world's most unusual accommodations: sleeping underwater in the Maldives, freezing in an ice hotel in Sweden, and hiding in a converted Dutch prison. Here's what I found, with the honest details that marketing materials leave out.

Underwater Hotels

1. Muraka at Conrad Maldives Rangali Island

Verified Stay

The Muraka is the world's first underwater hotel residence, and at $50,000 per night, it should be extraordinary — and it is, with caveats. The underwater bedroom sits 16 feet below sea level, and I fell asleep watching manta rays glide past the floor-to-ceiling acrylic windows. The upper level has a private infinity pool and sundeck. A personal butler and chef are included.

What they don't tell you: the underwater bedroom has no door (it's an open staircase from above), so it's not fully soundproof. You'll hear the gentle hum of the life support system. The Wi-Fi doesn't work underwater — you need to go upstairs to connect. And the $50,000 price tag doesn't include the speedboat transfer ($800 round trip from Malé). Still, falling asleep surrounded by bioluminescent plankton is a once-in-a-lifetime experience.

Key Details

  • Location: Rangali Island, South Ari Atoll, Maldives
  • Price: $50,000+ per night
  • Underwater bedroom: 16 feet below sea level
  • Included: Butler, chef, speedboat transfers
  • Not included: Wi-Fi underwater (only on upper level)
What I Liked
  • Falling asleep watching manta rays and reef fish
  • Private infinity pool with ocean views on upper level
  • Personal chef prepared whatever I wanted, whenever
  • Complete privacy — no other guests in sight
What Could Be Better
  • No Wi-Fi in the underwater bedroom
  • $800 speedboat transfer not included in rate
  • Open staircase means not fully soundproof

Ice Hotels

2. Icehotel 365 (Sweden)

Verified Stay

Icehotel 365 in Jukkasjärvi is open year-round thanks to solar-powered cooling that maintains -5°C even in summer. I visited in July when it was 25°C outside and -5°C inside — the temperature shock is part of the experience. Each room is carved entirely from ice and snow by different artists, so no two visits are the same.

The sleeping setup is surprisingly comfortable: a thermal sleeping bag on a reindeer hide over an ice bed. I slept warmly (rated to -30°C) but the bathroom is a 2-minute walk through an ice corridor — at -5°C, wearing only thermal underwear, this is an adventure at 3am. The ice bar serves cocktails in glasses made of ice, which sounds gimmicky but is genuinely fun. The Northern Lights visible from outside in winter are the real draw.

Key Details

  • Location: Jukkasjärvi, Swedish Lapland
  • Price: $300-800 per night depending on season
  • Temperature: Maintained at -5°C year-round
  • Sleeping: Thermal bag on reindeer hide over ice bed
  • Art suites: Redesigned annually by different artists
What I Liked
  • Each room is a unique art installation — genuinely one-of-a-kind
  • Thermal sleeping bags kept me warm even at -5°C
  • Ice bar is surprisingly enjoyable, not just a gimmick
  • Northern Lights visible in winter months
What Could Be Better
  • Bathroom is a 2-minute icy walk — brutal at 3am
  • Summer visits miss the Northern Lights entirely
  • Remote location — 16-hour train from Stockholm

Treehouse Hotels

3. Free Spirit Spheres (Canada)

Verified Stay

Three spherical treehouses suspended 15-150 feet above the forest floor in British Columbia's old-growth rainforest. I stayed in Eryn, suspended at 100 feet, accessed by a spiral staircase wrapped around the tree trunk. The interior is circular — bed in the center, windows all around — and at night, the forest canopy feels like it's embracing you.

The walkways connecting the spheres are narrow and sway slightly in the wind — not for anyone with vertigo. There's no electricity in the traditional sense; power comes from small solar panels, so there's no air conditioning (bring layers). The composting toilet is a 30-second walk across a swaying bridge. But waking up at dawn with birds singing inches from your window and mist threading through the trees — that's something no hotel can replicate.

Key Details

  • Location: Qualicum Beach, British Columbia, Canada
  • Price: $275-425 per night
  • Height: 15-150 feet above forest floor
  • Power: Solar panels, no air conditioning
  • Access: Spiral staircase wrapped around tree trunk
What I Liked
  • Waking up surrounded by old-growth rainforest canopy
  • Complete silence except for birds and wind
  • Each sphere has unique design and character
  • Eco-friendly construction with minimal environmental impact
What Could Be Better
  • No air conditioning — can be cold at night
  • Composting toilet requires a walk across swaying bridge
  • Swaying walkways may cause vertigo

Converted Buildings

4. Het Arresthuis (Netherlands)

Verified Stay

Formerly a prison in Roermond, Netherlands, Het Arresthuis retains original cell doors, barred windows, and corridor layouts. I stayed in Cell 17 — a former solitary confinement cell now fitted with a king bed, rainfall shower, and espresso machine. The irony of sipping artisan coffee behind a heavy steel door with a small observation window was not lost on me.

The common areas are fascinating: original prison exercise yards converted to gardens, the warden's office now a cocktail bar. The hotel has 105 rooms across different wings — the "luxury cells" have original barred windows, while the "comfort rooms" have been more extensively renovated and feel less like a prison. The on-site restaurant, previously the prison cafeteria, serves excellent Dutch-French cuisine. The location in Roermond is quiet and charming, with a Designer Outlet 10 minutes away.

Key Details

  • Location: Roermond, Netherlands
  • Price: $150-400 per night
  • Rooms: 105, in former prison wings
  • Original features: Cell doors, barred windows, corridors
  • Restaurant: Former prison cafeteria
What I Liked
  • Fascinating history — original prison features preserved throughout
  • Cocktail bar in former warden's office is atmospheric
  • Excellent restaurant with creative Dutch-French cuisine
  • "Luxury cell" concept is genuinely unique and Instagram-worthy
What Could Be Better
  • "Luxury cells" with barred windows may feel claustrophobic
  • Remote location — not ideal for city break
  • Heavy prison doors can be difficult to open with luggage

How to Book Unique Hotels

  • Book 6-12 months ahead: Most unique hotels have fewer than 20 rooms and sell out fast
  • Book directly: Unique properties often offer better rates and packages on their own sites
  • Read cancellation policies carefully: Many unique hotels have stricter cancellation terms than chains
  • Set weather expectations: Ice hotels are cold (obviously), treehouses can be damp, underwater rooms have humidity

Final Thoughts

Unique hotels aren't always comfortable — that's partly the point. The Icehotel's bathroom trek at 3am, the Free Spirit Sphere's swaying bridge, the Muraka's underwater hum — these are the details that make the experience memorable. If you want luxury comfort, book a chain. If you want a story to tell for the rest of your life, book one of these.
