Island travel in the Andamans hinges on ferry schedules across the Andaman Sea. Reaching Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep) requires flying into Port Blair (IXZ) first, followed by a 90-minute catamaran ferry ride across 38 km of open ocean.

Luxury resorts on Radhanagar Beach (Beach No. 7) charge premium rates for coconut grove villas. But if weather delays your morning ferry from Port Blair, you can lose a whole day of your stay waiting at Phoenix Bay Jetty.

Rates listed below were checked in July 2026 across major booking channels, reflecting off-peak monsoon rates and peak winter figures.

01

Taj Exotica Resort and Spa, Andamans

Radhanagar Beach (Beach No. 7), Havelock Island

Set across 46 acres of organic coconut plantations and native mahua trees, Taj Exotica sits directly adjacent to Radhanagar Beach. The resort constructed 58 luxury villas raised on stilts without felling a single canopy tree.

Villas span 147 square metres with sustainable padauk timber framing, pitched roofs, private pitch decks, and oversized bathrooms. Guests enjoy direct walking access to Radhanagar's pale white sands, an Olympic-length infinity pool, and guided mangrove night kayaking tours.

Indicative rates start at Rs 32,000 during monsoon low season and climb past Rs 85,000 per night during December and January peak. The luxury is authentic, but sandflies along the forest fringe at dusk require insect repellent.

02

Barefoot at Havelock

Beach No. 7 rainforest canopy, Havelock Island

Barefoot was Havelock's original eco-lodge on Radhanagar Beach. It features 31 thatched cottages built using indigenous timber, cane, and renewable bamboo tucked under rainforest foliage.

Cottages lack televisions and telephone landlines by design, encouraging guests to focus on rainforest birds and beach walks. A sandy path leads through mahua trees directly onto Radhanagar's swimming area.

Rates range from Rs 14,500 to Rs 32,000 per night. Air conditioning is provided in all luxury villas, but the open-nature setting means seeing small harmless geckos or forest insects in bathroom spaces is normal.

03

SeaShell Havelock

Govind Nagar Beach (Beach No. 2)

This is the itinerary mistake to skip: 1-night Havelock trips. Booking a single night on Havelock Island when traveling from Port Blair wastes money. Between ferry boarding times, luggage check-ins, and port transfers, a 1-night stay leaves you with less than four usable hours on the island.

If staying near the jetty district, SeaShell Havelock on Govind Nagar Beach offers wooden Andaman villa chalets with swimming pool access from Rs 9,800 to Rs 24,000 per night.

While Govind Nagar beach is rocky and unsuitable for swimming at low tide, the location puts you minutes from the scuba diving centers and local market cafes along Jetty Road.

What it costs to actually get there

Island isolation carries heavy logistical costs. After flying into Port Blair, standard private catamaran ferries (Makruzz or Nautika) cost between Rs 1,600 and Rs 2,500 per person for a one-way ticket to Havelock Island. That adds up to Rs 10,000 for a couple's round trip just in sea transport.

Once on Havelock, public transport is sparse. Auto-rickshaws dominate the roads, and a 15-minute ride from the jetty to Beach No. 7 can easily cost Rs 500. Renting a scooter costs around Rs 500 per day plus petrol, but requires confident driving skills on unlit forest roads at night.

When to go, and what changes

The Andaman Islands experience severe monsoon weather from late May through August. During these months, the sea turns rough, leading to frequent ferry cancellations. Water visibility drops significantly, making scuba diving at popular sites like Dixon's Pinnacle or Elephant Beach murky and unrewarding. Many beach shacks dismantle their structures entirely during the rainy season.

Peak season runs from November to March, offering calm seas, clear blue skies, and high diving visibility. However, room rates double during December and January, and ferry seats must be booked weeks in advance to avoid being stranded in Port Blair.

What the rate does not include

High-end island resorts import almost all their food and supplies from mainland India, making dining exceptionally expensive. A standard dinner at a resort restaurant often costs three times what it would on the mainland. Additionally, government environmental fees and port taxes apply to ferry tickets, and scuba diving certifications carry separate equipment rental and marine park fees.

Unlike mainland India, digital connectivity is rarely included in the room rate because it simply does not exist reliably. While luxury hotels advertise Wi-Fi, the actual satellite connection frequently drops or operates at dial-up speeds. Do not plan to work remotely from the islands unless you have verified dedicated leased-line access with your resort beforehand.

Planning Andaman island logistics

Plan a minimum of three nights on Havelock Island and two nights on Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep) to absorb island travel rhythms without rushing ferry transfers. Always keep one buffer night in Port Blair before your return flight to mainland India.

Practical Andaman island notes

  • Private catamarans (Makruzz, Nautika, Green Ocean) operate between Port Blair, Havelock, and Neil islands. Book ferry seats online weeks ahead during peak season.
  • Mobile network coverage on Havelock is limited. Airtel and BSNL function reasonably near town centers, but hotel Wi-Fi speed can be sluggish.
  • Radhanagar Beach swimming is permitted only within designated flags monitored by life guards until 5:00pm. Crocodiles are occasionally sighted in remote mangrove creeks.
  • Foreign nationals require no RAP (Restricted Area Permit) for major tourist islands, but passport registration is verified upon landing at Port Blair airport.
