Why the advertised rate is never the number you pay

An Indian hotel rate quoted on a booking page is a room rate. What lands on the card at checkout is the room rate plus tax, plus anything the property has decided is compulsory for your dates. The gap is routinely 15 to 40 per cent, and occasionally much more at peak season on the coast.

The tax slab, and the cliff at Rs 7,500

Indian hotel accommodation is taxed by nightly tariff band rather than by star rating. This calculator uses the two bands that apply to most travellers: the lower band up to Rs 7,500 a night, and the higher band above it. Those are the figures the tool assumes; slabs and thresholds do change, so confirm the current rate before you rely on a total.

The useful consequence is the step at the threshold. Because the higher band applies to the whole tariff rather than only the amount above the line, a room priced just over Rs 7,500 can cost noticeably more at checkout than one priced just under it, even though the advertised difference looked small. If two properties you are weighing sit either side of that line, the cheaper-looking one may be cheaper by more than it appears, and the dearer one by less. Put both into the fields above and the gap stops being theoretical.

What to put in the fees box

The mandatory extras field is where most of the surprise lives. Things worth hunting for before you book:

  • Compulsory meal plans. Common at resorts, wildlife lodges and island properties, where half board or full board is not optional and is quoted per person.
  • Gala dinner charges. Standard over Christmas and New Year in Goa and coastal Kerala, frequently thousands per head, and often disclosed only in the fine print of the rate you are booking.
  • Service charge. Legally voluntary in India, but still added to bills by some properties. You are entitled to ask for it to be removed.
  • Transfers. Airport or jetty transfers that read like an included service and are billed per journey.
  • Early check-in and late check-out. Charged as a fraction of a night at many hotels rather than given free.

Getting a real number before you book

Take the booking to the final confirmation screen without confirming it. That page shows the actual total including tax and compulsory items, which the search results page does not. For meal plans and gala charges, ask the property directly by email and keep the reply, because a written quote is what you argue from at checkout if the bill differs.

If your dates are flexible, run them through the seasonal rate checker first. Moving a booking by a fortnight changes the price more often than changing hotel does.
